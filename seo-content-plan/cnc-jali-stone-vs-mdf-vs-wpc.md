## 1. Introduction: The Evolution of the Indian Jali
The jali (perforated lattice screen) is one of Indian architecture’s most iconic inventions—providing natural ventilation, diffused daylighting, privacy, and cooling. Today, modern architects utilize three core materials for CNC jali fabrication:
1. **Natural Stone Jali** (Traditional Heritage & Exterior Facades)
2. **MDF / HDMR Jali** (Interior Partitions, False Ceilings, Dry Zone Decor)
3. **WPC / PVC Jali** (100% Waterproof, Termite-proof, Exterior & Semi-wet Zones)

## 3. Best Use Cases for Each Material

### A. When to Specify Natural Stone Jali
* **Historic Restoration & Royal Facades:** When creating Mughal, Rajasthani, or Indo-Saracenic architectural aesthetics.
* **Direct Outdoor Exposure:** Endures 48°C desert heat and torrential monsoon rain without warping or yellowing.
* **Pooja Mandir Sacred Enclosures:** Authentic white marble jali provides sacred tranquility.

### B. When to Specify MDF / HDMR Jali
* **Interior Living Room Room Dividers:** Where lightweight fabrication allows sliding partitions and ceiling hangings.
* **Complex Multi-Color Finishes:** Allows mirror-finish high-gloss PU lacquers, metallic brass coatings, or matte white duco paint.
* **Cost-Optimized Interior Renovations:** High visual impact at budget-friendly manufacturing costs.

### C. When to Specify WPC Jali
* **Wet & Humid Environments:** Bathroom partitions, washbasin privacy screens, and rooftop pergolas.
* **Exterior Boundary Wall Screens:** Lightweight alternative to stone where masonry cannot bear heavy point loads.

---

## 4. Engineering & Frame Mounting Guidelines
* **Natural Stone:** Must be embedded in structural steel/aluminum box frames with 12mm deep perimeter grooves and expansion joints.
* **MDF & WPC:** Fixed using solid hardwood or MS tubular perimeter framework to prevent bowing over spans exceeding 6 feet.
